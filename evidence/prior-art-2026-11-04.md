## C1 — Problem framing: the unit of decision is the executable order, not the asset weight

### (a) Claim in one defensible sentence
At retail capital the decision variable is the executable order rather than the portfolio weight, and the breadth of *estimation* (a 12M-row daily panel) is deliberately decoupled from the breadth of *holding* (4 investable sleeves + cash), because holding breadth is capped by the 100-share board lot and the 5 CNY commission floor, not by conviction.

### (b) Closest work, with exact location
**Baule, R. (2010), "Optimal portfolio selection for the small investor considering risk and transaction costs", *OR Spectrum* 32:61–76.** DOI 10.1007/s00291-008-0152-5, open access.
- URL: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00291-008-0152-5 (PDF: https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00291-008-0152-5.pdf)
- Abstract (p.61): *"A direct application of classical portfolio selection theory is problematic for the small investor because of transaction costs in the form of bank and broker fees. In particular, **minimum fees force the investor to choose a comparatively rather small selection of assets**."*
- Eq. (5), §2 (p.64): cost per position `c(x_i) = max{a, b·x_i}·1{x_i>0}` with `a, b > 0` — a minimum fee per position plus a linear component, i.e. structurally the same object as the 5 CNY floor.
- §3.2, Table 2 (p.71): optimal number of assets as a function of invested wealth — `n*=1` at 1,000–2,000 EUR, `n*=2` at 5,000, `n*=3` at 10,000, `n*=6` at 20,000, `n*=10` at 50,000, `n*=14` at 100,000 (with average transaction size rising from 2,000 to 7,100 EUR).
- Conclusion (p.74): *"Transaction costs force small investors to concentrate their money on a comparatively small selection of securities."*

**Du, J., D. Huang, Y.-J. Liu, Y. Shi, A. Subrahmanyam, H. Zhang (Dec 2022), "Retail Investors and Momentum".**
- URL: https://anderson.ucla.edu/sites/default/files/document/2023-04/4.19.23%20A%20Subra%20paper%20asharemom8%20(002).pdf
- §1.2 "Price-based constraint" (MS p.11): *"in the Chinese market, a strict roundlot rule applies such that investors must buy or sell stocks in units of 100 shares. The direct consequence of this rule is to impose an upper limit on the stock price an investment can accommodate."* And, on why 100 shares is not enough resolution: *"a stock with a sufficiently high price such that a small investor could just about buy 100 shares would mean the ability to either take a 100 share position or no position in the stock. In practice, investors likely would want the ability to adjust their exposure to the stock. For instance, if the high-priced stock outperforms the investors' other positions, the investor may want to rebalance their portfolio by selling just a portion of their position in the high priced stock."* They therefore use **400 shares** as the practical limit.
- Table 1 Panel A (p.9): small investors (average portfolio < 200k CNY, 43.5% of accounts) hold **3.4 stocks**, mean/median trade size **17.5k / 10.4k CNY**, trade 13.6 times/month; large investors (> 500k) hold 8.2 stocks, median trade 67.4k CNY.

**Jones, C. M., D. Shi, X. Zhang, X. Zhang (2025), "Retail Trading and Return Predictability in China", *JFQA* 60(1):68–104.** DOI 10.1017/S0022109024000085.
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022109024000085
- §I (p.70) and Table 1: the exchange's own account-size stratification puts **RT1 = accounts below 100,000 CNY** — 31.4 million accounts, the single largest group, and the group that contains this project's entire 50k–100k capital band.
- §V.B, Table 8 discussion (p.94): the trading-cost component of annualised return-on-investment is **−1.38% for RT1** (−1.80% for RT2, −0.41% for institutions); RT1–RT4 have neither stock-selection nor market-timing ability.

**Brennan, M. J. (1975), "The optimal number of securities in a risky asset portfolio when there are fixed costs of transacting: theory and some empirical results", *JFQA* 10(3):483–496** — the origin of the "fixed costs ⇒ optimal number of holdings" question, cited in Baule (2010) ref. list p.75. `待全文核验` (not opened).

### (c) Which part of the claim already exists
Two of the three parts exist, stated plainly:

1. *Holding breadth is capped by the fee floor, not by conviction.* This **is** Baule's abstract sentence. His §3.2 turns it into a quantitative wealth→`n*` map, and his conclusion gives it as the general lesson.
2. *Lot indivisibility destroys rebalancing resolution at small capital in China.* Du et al. state it explicitly, including the "100 shares = all-or-nothing" argument and the 400-share practical threshold. Their Table 1 shows real Chinese small accounts behaving exactly as the mechanism predicts: 3.4 names, ~10k CNY median tickets.
3. The empirical setting itself (accounts under 100k CNY, 31.4M of them, losing to costs) is documented by Jones et al.

What does **not** exist in this family: nobody writes the decision variable as *the order* (an integer number of lots with an admissibility test), and nobody makes "estimation breadth ≠ holding breadth" an explicit design principle with a measurement consequence. But a framing is only as good as the measurement it licenses, and the measurement is C4 — where (see below) the novelty is narrower than the framing implies.

### (d) Substantive difference, and the conditions it depends on
The difference is real but it is narrow, and it is *not* the one the claim advertises.

- **Baule's feasible set is never empty.** Quantities are continuous, there is no lot size (`lots` appear only as a cited literature strand, §1 p.63: "costs or lots mainly under computational issues"), no turnover cap, no gate. Given a minimum fee, his optimiser always has an interior answer: hold fewer names, trade larger tickets. At the project's capital (50,000–100,000 CNY ≈ 4,700–9,300 EUR at the Dec-2007 rate Baule's fee table is calibrated to) his answer is **n\* ≈ 2–3**, with average transaction size ≈ 2,500–3,300 EUR ≈ 27,000–35,000 CNY.
- **The project's feasible set can be empty.** A 100-share lot at 1–1.5 CNY is 100–140 CNY; the one-way all-in infimum is 7.6 bp; a sleeve's monthly budget is 6,250 CNY at 50k NAV. Under a hard gate the choice can be "no admissible order", which no continuous-weight model can express. **That** is the new object, and it is C4's, not C1's.
- **GP asserts the opposite of the project's premise.** Gârleanu & Pedersen (JF 2013; NBER WP 15205, https://www.nber.org/papers/w15205), after eq. (10) on PDF p.9: *"A larger absolute risk aversion can also be viewed as a smaller investor, for whom transaction costs play a smaller role, and who therefore trades closer to her aim."* Under a purely quadratic cost this is correct; it is exactly the assumption the project's fixed floor breaks. The fixed-cost literature says so in as many words (see C3).
- **Conditions the claim depends on.** (i) The 5 CNY minimum is regulatory, not incidental: A-share and fund commissions are charged at 5 CNY when the computed fee falls below it, and the regulator has moved to prohibit brokers from waiving it ("免5"), see 财联社 2026-10-26 https://www.cls.cn/detail/2160172 — so the gate is realistic, but a single rule change voids the whole construction. (ii) The claim is only interesting if the gate binds, which the project's own evidence says it does at 50k (0.1% of cells). (iii) It assumes lot size stays 100 shares for these codes.

### (e) Verdict
**部分已有**

### (f) Recommendation
**Adapt.**
- Adopt Baule's *decomposition* as the reporting frame: total cost = transaction cost + **risk cost** (the cost of holding a portfolio that deviates from the unconstrained optimum because it has too few names). This is the one idea that converts "the trade is infeasible" into a number that can be compared against the alternative of holding one more name.
- Adopt Baule's Table 2 as a **falsification target**: his map says 2–3 names at this capital. The project proposes 4 sleeves + cash. Either show why 4 is admissible at 50k under the gate (the answer will probably be "it isn't at 50k and it is at 100k", which is the project's own step function), or reduce the sleeve count.
- Cite Brennan (1975) as the origin and Du et al. (2022) for the lot mechanism; do not present either as new.
- **Licence / version.** Baule (2010) is open access under *Creative Commons Attribution Noncommercial* (stated on p.75) — figures and text reusable non-commercially **with attribution**. **No code is released**, so nothing to adopt; the heuristic is described in §2–§3 and cannot be lifted, and in any case it does not handle integer lots. Do not port it.
- Reject the framing sentence as written ("…is not the asset weight but the executable order"): it is a re-description of a 1975 question plus a 2010 answer. Keep the *consequence* (an empty feasible set) and drop the fanfare.

## C2 — Mechanism: panel-layer estimation → ≤4-parameter combiner → sleeve actions; NN only as a comparison arm

### (a) Claim in one defensible sentence
Estimate (μ, σ, expected shortfall) per sleeve once at the panel layer from a cross-sectional model, map those to sleeve actions through a combiner with at most 4 fitted parameters, and admit a neural network only as a panel-layer comparison arm — never as the portfolio former.

### (b) Closest work, with exact location
**Brandt, M. W., P. Santa-Clara, R. Valkanov (2009), "Parametric Portfolio Policies", *RFS* 22(9):3411–3443** (NBER WP 10996, https://www.nber.org/papers/w10996). PDF p.5:
- *"We parameterize the portfolio weight of each stock as a function of the firm's characteristics and estimate the coefficients of the portfolio policy by maximizing the utility that would have been obtained by implementing the policy over the sample period."*
- *"We optimize the entire portfolio by choosing only a few parameters θ. This parsimony reduces the risk of in-sample overfitting since the coefficients will only deviate from zero if the respective characteristics offer an interesting combination of return and risk consistently across stocks and through time."*
- Crucially, the same page argues **against** a layered design: BSCV claim their approach avoids *"the auxiliary, yet very difficult, step of modeling the joint distribution of returns and characteristics."*

**Gu, S., B. Kelly, D. Xiu (2020), "Empirical Asset Pricing via Machine Learning", *RFS* 33(5):2223–2273** (NBER WP 25398, https://www.nber.org/papers/w25398). Verified in full text: the phrase "transaction cost" occurs **zero** times; Table 8 reports turnover only — *"For NN1 through NN5, turnover is consistently between 110% and 130% per month, and turnover for tree-based methods and penalized regression is slightly higher"* (reversal decile 172.6%/month, size decile 22.9%/month). No net-of-cost performance anywhere. p.43: *"While value-weight portfolios are less sensitive to trading cost considerations, it is perhaps more…"*

**Leippold, M., Q. Wang, W. Zhou (2022), "Machine learning in the Chinese stock market", *JFE* 145(2):64–82.** DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2021.08.017. Full accepted MS: https://www.zora.uzh.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/1cc6eafc-5c0c-41b2-97f7-aaad9eac649f/content
- §4.4 "Transaction costs" (MS p.16): three components (commission, stamp tax, slippage); *"the commission fee for institutional investors was around 5 bps in 2012, then quickly decreased. In recent years, the commission fee is usually 2-3 bps for retail investors and even lower for institutional investors. The stamp tax has been set to 10 bps since 2008 and is collected unilaterally from sellers."*
- slippage from TWAP/VWAP over the first 30 minutes: *"On average, the total deviation of the TWAP and VWAP from the open price is around 10 bps after accounting for both buying and selling"*; *"A back-of-the-envelope calculation indicates that 25 bps might be a reasonable estimate of transaction cost in the Chinese stock market during normal times… we take a conservative approach by considering trading costs of 20, 40, 60, and 80 bps."*
- Table 10 (MS p.18): applied as **proportional shocks**, NN4 long-short Sharpe 2.91 → 2.34 at 80 bp round trip (2.76 at 20 bp); long-only 1.68 → 1.46 at 80 bp; *"the portfolios still provide a considerable and economically significant performance."*
- Table 9 "Market Capacity" = 5% of trading volume — an **upper** bound on AUM.

**Avramov, D., S. Cheng, L. Metzker (2023), "Machine Learning vs. Economic Restrictions: Evidence from Stock Return Predictability", *Management Science* 69(5):2587–2619.** PDF p.16 (= journal p.2601): ex-post break-even transaction cost, *"we can therefore infer a break-even transaction cost of 0.94% (1.12%, 0.53%, 0.48%) for the long-short portfolio in the full sample and 0.36% (0.34%, 0.54%, 0.26%)"* excluding microcaps, against monthly long-short turnover of 87–98% (GKX) up to 163–168% (CPZ).

### (c) Which part of the claim already exists
All three pieces, separately:
- The **≤4-parameter combiner** is BSCV's contribution, with BSCV's own overfitting argument reproduced almost verbatim in the project's rationale.
- **NN vs linear at the panel layer, compared, with costs** is done for the US by GKX turnover + Avramov break-even costs, and for China by Leippold et al. with proportional cost shocks up to 80 bp — including the long-only variant, which is the one that applies to this project.
- **μ, σ as the sufficient statistics per sleeve, then a rule** is the standard two-step in every practitioner pipeline; it is not a contribution.

What does not exist: the *architectural prohibition* — a rule that a network may propose panel forecasts but may never emit weights. That is a self-imposed discipline. It may be good engineering; it is not prior-art-new.

### (d) Substantive difference, and the conditions it depends on
- **The two-layer split contradicts BSCV's stated rationale.** BSCV argue the single-layer parametric policy is *better* precisely because it skips the joint-distribution step. A project that splits the layers owes an argument. The available argument is a sample-size one and it is already recorded in the project's own state (`nn-belongs-at-panel-layer`, seq 41): ~900 basket-months at 50 observations per parameter ≈ 18 parameters at the holding layer, so a network is rejected by construction, while the panel layer has 12,008,787 rows where a network is admissible. That is a defensible argument — but it must be made explicitly, because a reader who knows BSCV will read the two-layer design as a regression, not an advance.
- **All comparators are continuous-weight, proportional-cost, many-name.** Leippold et al. rebalance value-weighted portfolios of hundreds of stocks at monthly frequency with 20–80 bp proportional costs. There is no per-order minimum, no lot, no 50k account. The correct statement of the difference is: *Leippold et al. show ML survives proportional costs in China; they do not show it survives the 5 CNY floor at 50k, and their setting cannot express the question.* That is a difference in **object**, and it is the same difference as C1(d).
- **Conditions.** The comparison arm only means something if it is compared on the *same* net-of-cost footing as the linear arm. GKX compares gross. If the project compares an NN panel layer to a linear panel layer and both feed the same combiner, the cost structure cancels and the comparison measures forecast quality only — which is fine, but then it is a forecast-accuracy experiment, not an economic one, and it should be labelled as such.

### (e) Verdict
**部分已有**

### (f) Recommendation
**Adapt.**
- Keep the split, but **justify it by parameter count, not by architecture**: 18-parameter ceiling at the holding layer is the reason, and it is measurable. Record BSCV as the rejected alternative and say why.
- Adopt Leippold et al. §4.4 as the **China cost benchmark for the panel layer** (2–3 bp commission retail, 10 bp unilateral stamp tax on sellers, ~10 bp slippage round trip, 25 bp "reasonable normal-times"), and note that these are *proportional* numbers that already exclude the project's 5 CNY floor — so the panel layer can use them while the holding layer must use the floor.
- State the NN arm's role as *forecast-quality comparison*, not as an economic claim. Do not claim Leippold/Avramov as net-of-cost support for a 50k order-level strategy; they are not.
- **Licence / version.** No official code located for BSCV, GKX or Leippold et al.; do not claim otherwise. If the NN arm is built, the reference implementations circulating for GKX are third-party reimplementations of unstated licence — treat as `待全文核验` before reuse.

## C3 — Objective: net CNY under turnover ≤ 6 and a drawdown ceiling at construction; 3-state action grid

### (a) Claim in one defensible sentence
Maximise net CNY after costs subject to annual turnover ≤ 6 and a drawdown ceiling imposed at construction time (January 95% VaR ≤ d_max/6 plus a state throttle), with the per-sleeve action space discretised to 3 states because the cost gate destroys resolution below that.

### (b) Closest work, with exact location
**Fixed costs ⇒ no-trade region, and the non-zero action is a bulk trade to the target.**
Altarovici, A., J. Muhle-Karbe, H. M. Soner, "Asymptotics for Fixed Transaction Costs", arXiv:1306.2802 (https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.2802), §1, PDF p.2:
- *"Proportional costs lead to infinitely many small transactions. In contrast, fixed costs only allow for a finite number of trades. Then, the optimal policy again corresponds to a no-trade region. **However, since trades of all sizes are now penalized equally, rebalancing takes place by a bulk trade to the optimal frictionless target in the middle of the no-trade region.**"*
- Same page: *"fixed costs destroy the favorable scaling properties that usually allow to reduce the dimensionality of the problem for utilities with constant relative or absolute risk aversion. In particular, the boundaries of the no-trade region are no longer constant"*, and, twice: existing fixed-cost asymptotics *"do not allow to study how the impact of fixed trading costs depends on the size of the investor under consideration."*

**Fixed costs dominate at low wealth, and the region changes shape with wealth.**
Altarovici, A., M. Reppen, H. M. Soner (2017), "Optimal Consumption and Investment with Fixed and Proportional Transaction Costs", *SIAM J. Control Optim.* 55(3):1673–1710, DOI 10.1137/15M1053633 (PDF: https://soner.princeton.edu/sites/g/files/toruqf4216/files/soner/files/92-fixed-costs.pdf).
- §1, PDF p.1674: *"The main drawback to fixed costs from a modeling point of view is that they destroy the favorable scaling properties… In particular, **the no-trade region is no longer a cone**, even in the simplest settings with constant investment opportunities as well as constant absolute or relative risk aversion."*
- §5.7, PDF p.1703: *"**At low wealth levels, fixed transaction costs are the predominant cost consideration, whereas at higher wealth levels proportional costs prevail.** … the no-trade region appears to interpolate between the elliptic shape associated to fixed costs at low wealth levels and the parallelogram shape associated to proportional costs at high wealth levels. Note also that the total transaction costs are comparatively smaller for a wealthier agent, resulting in smaller no-trade regions in relation to wealth."*

**Proportional-cost baseline, for contrast.** Gârleanu & Pedersen (2013), NBER WP 15205, Proposition 2 "Trade Partially Towards the Aim": `x_t = x_{t-1} + Λ^{-1}A_xx(aim_t − x_{t-1})` (eq. 7), `x_t = (1 − a/Λ)x_{t-1} + (a/Λ)aim_t` (eq. 10), scalar rate `a` in eq. (9); and *"the optimal portfolio always rebalances by fixed fraction towards the aim (i.e., the trading rate is independent of the current portfolio x_{t-1} or past portfolios)"* (PDF p.9). A quadratic cost means **no** no-trade region and **no** floor — the structural opposite of this project's problem.

**Drawdown ceiling at construction.** Grossman, S. J., Z. Zhou (1993), "Optimal Investment Strategies for Controlling Drawdowns", *Math. Finance* 3(3):241–276 — exact analytical solution for a maximal-drawdown constraint, but in a **continuous-time, one-risky-one-riskless** setting (characterised in Chekhlov et al. 2005 §1.2, PDF p.14). Chekhlov, A., S. Uryasev, M. Zabarankin (2005), "Drawdown Measure in Portfolio Optimization", *IJTAF* 8(1):13–58, DOI 10.1142/S0219024905002767 (PDF: https://math.columbia.edu/~chekhlov/ChekhlovUryasevZabarankin--03-2004.pdf) — drawdown functionals (CDD/CDaR) reduce to **linear programming**, so a drawdown ceiling is imposable at construction; §1.1 (PDF p.13) records where real ceilings come from: *"An account may be shut down if a 20% drawdown is breached. A warning is issued if an account in a 15% drawdown."*

**Turnover constraint as an economic restriction.** Avramov, Cheng & Metzker (2023) §5, imposition of turnover/macro restrictions on ML portfolios and the resulting break-even costs (PDF p.16). No-trade-band results as such: Davis & Norman (1990) *Math. OR* 15(4):676–713 and Constantinides (1986) *JPE* 94(4):842–862 — `待全文核验` (no free full text located; the Davis–Norman result is reliably reported in Altarovici et al. 2017 §1, PDF p.1674: *"Davis and Norman [13] showed that trading boundaries can be determined numerically by solving a free-boundary problem"*).

### (c) Which part of the claim already exists
- **Turnover-constrained construction**: fully established, and in the ML-portfolio literature specifically.
- **Drawdown ceiling at construction**: fully established (Grossman–Zhou analytically; CDaR by LP). The specific "January 95% VaR ≤ d_max/6" scaling is a local heuristic, not a studied object, and it is a *one-month VaR*, not a drawdown — a fact already flagged in the project's own open objection F-113a8e8d-4.
- **Wealth-dependence of the fixed-cost policy, and the qualitative change at low wealth**: established. Altarovici–Reppen–Soner say it in one sentence: at low wealth the fixed fee dominates and the region is no longer a cone.
- **Discrete action sets** from integer constraints: established in the MILP literature (see C1/C4: Mansini & Speranza 1999, Kellerer et al. 2000, Jobst et al. 2001).

What does not exist: deriving the grid from a **measured** admissibility test rather than from an optimisation. That is C4's content again.

### (d) Substantive difference, and the conditions it depends on
- **The theory's grid is binary, not ternary.** Under a pure fixed cost the optimal non-zero action is a **bulk trade all the way to the target** (Altarovici–Muhle-Karbe–Soner, quoted above). The project proposes three states — 0, half, full — and the "half" state has **no support in the fixed-cost literature**. Under fixed-plus-proportional costs the target is the *middle* of the no-trade region and the trade goes to an inner boundary, which produces a continuum, not a half-step. The project should treat **{0, full} as the theoretical baseline** and the 3-state grid as the alternative to be tested, not the reverse. This is a cheap, decisive experiment and it should be run before the grid is frozen.
- **Scale is the crux and the theory does not resolve it at this size.** Altarovici et al.'s statement that the region "is no longer a cone" is the qualitative half; the quantitative half — how many orders per year survive at 50k CNY — is not in the theory, because the theory has neither lots nor a 5 CNY floor. That gap is genuine.
- **Conditions.** (i) The drawdown proxy (Jan 95% VaR ≤ d_max/6) is a *construction* constraint in a model, and the project's own objection F-113a8e8d-4 stands: a realised-drawdown check cannot enforce a prospective ceiling. Grossman–Zhou's exact solution applies only in one dimension with continuous trading. (ii) Turnover ≤ 6/yr is an exogenous budget, so it interacts with the fee floor in a way no cited paper models: a fixed fee makes you want *fewer, larger* trades, while a turnover cap makes you want *fewer, smaller* ones. That interaction is not in the literature I found, and it is closer to a real contribution than the grid is. (iii) 3 states × 4 sleeves = 81 states/month; at ~131 months this is a 10,611-cell evaluation, and the number of *non-empty* cells is the C4 measurement.

### (e) Verdict
**部分已有**

### (f) Recommendation
**Adapt, with one change of baseline.**
- Replace the 3-state grid with **{0, full}** as the null and make "half" the treatment. The fixed-cost theory predicts 2 states; if a 3rd state earns nothing out of sample, the project has a cleaner result, and if it earns something, that is a finding against theory and worth more than the grid itself.
- Keep the turnover cap and the drawdown ceiling as exogenous budgets, and cite them as such (Avramov et al. for turnover; Chekhlov et al. for the LP-imposable ceiling). Do not present either as a new objective.
- Record the turnover-cap × fee-floor interaction (§d-ii) as the open question in this section: it is the only object here that I could not find treated anywhere.
- **Licence / version.** No code released with Grossman–Zhou, Chekhlov et al., or Altarovici et al.; the CDaR LP formulation is in the paper's appendix but not packaged. Nothing to adopt.

## C4 — Measurement: "feasibility" as a first-class research output

### (a) Claim in one defensible sentence
Report feasibility as a primary research output — minimum executable order size, cleared-cell share, and count of executable months — so that "this question is not measurable at this capital" is a distinct verdict from "no edge found".

### (b) Closest work, with exact location
**Baule (2010), §3.3 and Conclusion** — the closest published analogue, and it goes further than the project's framing admits.
- §3.3 "Direct investment versus tailor-made products" (p.71): *"this section compares a direct investment against popular alternative investment vehicles… the cost structures of (classical) mutual index funds, exchange-traded index funds, and index certificates can directly be compared"*, done **as a function of invested wealth**.
- Conclusion (p.75): *"Our analysis also provides guidelines for choosing between direct investments in stocks or in alternative vehicles. **Direct investment is advantageous if the sum of associated risk and transaction costs is smaller than the annual costs of the alternative investment products.**"*
- Table 2 (p.71) is literally a feasibility map: wealth → optimal number of assets → relative transaction cost → relative risk cost → their ratio.

**Capacity literature — the upper bound, i.e. the mirror problem.**
- Leippold et al. (2022) Table 9 "Market Capacity": 5% of trading volume, per strategy — an upper bound on AUM.
- "Capacity Analysis for Equity Funds" (ANU open research repository, https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/server/api/core/bitstreams/8038e723-975d-4222-9608-9a3a544d8d34/content): *"we refine how capacity is defined as effective capacity, which is the amount of assets under management (AUM) at which a fund becomes unable to make further active investment"*.

**Practitioner statement of the exact question.** Concretum Group, "Estimating the Capacity of a Trading Strategy", https://concretumgroup.com/estimating-the-capacity-of-a-trading-strategy/: *"capacity is best understood as a three-way problem… How much capital can we run a signal on? How much capital should we run a signal on? **What minimum capital does the signal require to run?**"* Blog, not peer-reviewed: it names the question and offers no measurement.

**Statistical feasibility — a different axis, often confused with this one.** Bailey, D. H., M. López de Prado, "The Deflated Sharpe Ratio" (*J. Portfolio Management* 40(5), 2014): *"corrects for two leading sources of performance inflation: Selection bias under multiple testing and non-Normally distributed returns"*; *"DSR is a PSR where the rejection threshold is adjusted to reflect the multiplicity of trials"*. And Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado, Zhu, "The Probability of Backtest Overfitting": PBO = *"the conditional probability that this strategy underperforms the median OOS while remaining optimal IS"*, plus MinTRL/MinBTL from the same group. **None of these asks whether an order of the required size can clear a fixed cost floor.**

### (c) Which part of the claim already exists
- **"What minimum capital does this need" as a legitimate research output**: exists, named explicitly by practitioners, and answered with a wealth-indexed map by Baule for the direct-stock-vs-fund question.
- **Wealth-dependent *instrument-class* viability**: exists. Baule's Table 2 + §3.3 is exactly "which vehicle is viable at which wealth", and it concludes that at small wealth the fund/ETF wrapper beats direct holdings. This project's one-lot drag table (511010 at 28.27% of 50k vs 159920 at 0.28%) is the same finding in a different instrument taxonomy — and the project's choice to trade ETFs is therefore the *predicted* answer, not a discovery.
- **"Not measurable at this capital" as a distinct verdict from "no edge"**: does not exist as a published statement I could find. Every capacity paper reports a ceiling; every statistical-power paper reports a trial budget. No paper reports "the question cannot be posed at this account size".

What does not exist: **the specific metric** — cleared-cell share over (month × instrument) at a given NAV and tolerance, and the count of executable months. I found no paper, working paper, or code base that computes this.

### (d) Substantive difference, and the conditions it depends on
- **Optimum vs admissibility.** Baule's map reports `n*(wealth)` — an *optimum* inside a never-empty feasible set. The project reports an *admissibility* map — which orders exist at all — which is a different object, and the only object in this whole report that no cited work can express. Baule's optimiser is never told "you cannot buy this"; the project's is.
- **Direction.** Capacity work bounds capital from above (Leippold's 5%-of-volume, ANU's effective-capacity AUM). The project bounds it from below. The two are not duals: the upper bound is about market impact, the lower bound about fixed fees, and no paper I found states the lower bound formally.
- **Conditions.** (i) The metric is only interesting where the gate binds: 0.1% cleared cells at 50k, 93.9% at 75k, 99.7% at 100k is a near step function — a striking shape, and the step is the result. (ii) The metric depends on the tolerance, which is a *choice*: at 7.6 bp nothing clears by construction, so the informative range is 10–30 bp and the choice must be justified, not assumed. (iii) It is a model-free, backward-looking count: it says what *would have been* executable on the historical panel, not what a broker will fill. That is fine, but say so.

### (e) Verdict
**部分已有**

### (f) Recommendation
**Adapt — and this is where the project should concentrate.**
- The framing of C4 is the project's strongest asset and its weakest claim at the same time: the *question* is standard practice (Baule answers a version of it; capacity practitioners name it), the *metric* is not. Build the contribution claim on the metric, not the question.
- Adopt Baule's **risk-cost vs transaction-cost decomposition** as the reporting algebra: it lets a cleared-cell-share table sit beside an "if you insist on trading anyway, here is the risk cost" number, which is the only honest way to answer "so what?" when 99.9% of cells are blocked.
- Do **not** claim novelty for "minimum viable capital". Cite Concretum for the practitioner statement, Leippold Table 9 / ANU for the upper bound, and Baule for the wealth-indexed map — then say plainly that the cleared-cell share is the new part.
- **Licence / version.** No code. Baule CC-BY-NC as in C1(f). Nothing to adopt.

## C5 — Execution: post-rounding cost gate + hash-chained ledger with per-class settlement

### (a) Claim in one defensible sentence
Gate each order on the cost recomputed from the frozen fee schedule applied to the **final executable** notional after lot rounding (not on the target weight), and record every decision in an append-only hash-chained ledger with per-instrument-class settlement (T+0 bond/commodity, T+1 equity/overseas) in which any recomputed-cost mismatch is a hard error rather than a silent correction.

### (b) Closest work, with exact location
**Round-before-cost, as an established modelling step.** Kellerer, H., R. Mansini, M. G. Speranza (2000), "Selecting Portfolios with Fixed Costs and Minimum Transaction Lots", *Annals of OR* 99:287–304, DOI 10.1023/A:1019279918596: *"different mixed-integer linear programming models dealing with fixed costs and possibly minimum lots are introduced… heuristic procedures, based on the construction and optimal solution of mixed integer subproblems."* Same family: Mansini & Speranza (1999) *EJOR* DOI 10.1016/S0377-2217(98)00252-5; Speranza (1996); Jobst, Horniman, Lucas & Mitra (2001) *Quantitative Finance* 1(5):489–501. In these, the lot constraint is *inside* the optimisation, so cost is by construction evaluated on the executable quantity. `待全文核验` for the specific claim that any of them *names* a post-rounding re-evaluation step; the abstracts establish the modelling object, not the phrasing.

**Hash-chained tamper-evident logs.** Crosby, S. A., D. S. Wallach (2009), "Efficient Data Structures for Tamper-Evident Logging", *USENIX Security* (https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/sec09/tech/full_papers/crosby.pdf). Abstract: *"The logger must be able to prove that individual logged events are still present, and that the log, as seen now, is consistent with how it was seen in the past."* And on the baseline the project actually uses: *"Where a **classic hash chain** might require an 800 MB trace to prove that a randomly chosen event is in a log with 80 million events, our prototype returns a 3 KB proof with the same semantics."* Their structure is a Merkle history tree; the project's `sha256(prev | seq | kind | payload)` chain is the classic baseline they improve on.

**Content-addressed experiment provenance, read from source.** DVC, canonical repo `treeverse/dvc`, licence **Apache-2.0**, ~15,957 stars, branch `main`, 591 files at HEAD (https://github.com/treeverse/dvc).
- `dvc/stage/serialize.py:143` `def to_single_stage_lockfile(stage: "Stage", **kwargs) -> dict:` builds the lock entry; `:160-161` `if item.hash_name not in LEGACY_HASH_NAMES: ret[item.PARAM_HASH] = "md5"` records the hash *algorithm* beside the digest; `:174` `ret.update(_serialize_hi_to_dict(item.hash_info))` writes md5 + size; `:186-193` assemble `OrderedDict([("cmd", …)])` + deps + sorted params + outs; `:195` `def to_lockfile(stage: "PipelineStage", **kwargs) -> dict: return {stage.name: to_single_stage_lockfile(stage, **kwargs)}`.
- `dvc/repo/index.py:123` `hash_info=ohi if (ohi and ohi.name == hash_name) else None` — **a hash whose algorithm differs is silently dropped, not raised**; `:199-201` `if not out.hash_info or dep.fs.version_aware:` goes down a recompute/refresh path.

### (c) Which part of the claim already exists
- **Append-only hash-chained tamper-evident record**: textbook since Haber–Stornetta; Crosby–Wallach is the canonical engineering treatment and explicitly names the classic hash chain as the thing it improves. `核心已有`.
- **Content-addressed provenance with recorded hash algorithm and content digest**: DVC, in production, Apache-2.0. `核心已有`.
- **Per-class settlement (T+0/T+1) as a ledger invariant**: standard clearing-and-settlement bookkeeping; the project's `LedgerError` on "sell > held" (`newquant/ledger.py:200`) and on unknown corporate actions (`:217`) is conventional.
- **Cost evaluated on executable quantity**: implied by every lot-constrained MILP (above).

What does not exist in the sources I read: the *specific invariant* "recompute every fill's cost from a frozen schedule and treat any mismatch as an error", applied to a **trading** ledger. DVC's analogue is stricter-than-nothing only for content hashes, and on algorithm mismatch it goes the **other way** — it drops the hash and, without it, refreshes the entry (`dvc/repo/index.py:123`, `:199-201`). So the project's I-2/I-6 is genuinely stricter than the closest maintained implementation.

### (d) Substantive difference, and the conditions it depends on
- **The difference is a policy choice, not an invention.** Both systems record a digest per artefact. DVC treats a mismatch as "unknown, recompute"; `newquant/ledger.py:96` raises `LedgerError("hash mismatch (record edited)")`. That is a defensible choice for a research ledger whose purpose is to make silent edits impossible — and it is an **engineering** choice. It does not belong in a list of research contributions.
- **The scale argument cuts against complexity.** The project's record volume is ~40 orders/year (`D:\newquant\.quant\state.md`), i.e. hundreds of records, not 80 million. A classic hash chain is sufficient; Crosby–Wallach's O(log n) proofs solve a problem this ledger will not have for decades. Adopting their tree would be over-engineering.
- **Conditions.** (i) The gate is only well-defined if the fee schedule is frozen and versioned — the project does freeze it (`etf_std`, hash `d6a6e438e32e818f…` in `evidence/feasibility-d6a6e438.md`), which is what makes the recompute check meaningful. (ii) Recompute-on-load must be deterministic in rounding: `Decimal` arithmetic and lot integer amounts are already used (`newquant/order.py:89-93` return `no_admissible_size` / `below_cost_gate` with the `Decimal` cost attached), and any float in the fee path breaks I-2. (iii) The mutual consistency of "suppressed order is a record" (I-8) with replay determinism (I-6) is the part worth testing, not the hashing.

### (e) Verdict
**部分已有**

### (f) Recommendation
**Adopt the vocabulary, reject the contribution claim.**
- Adopt Crosby–Wallach's terms — **tamper-evident**, **consistent with how it was seen in the past** — so I-6 reads as a recognised property rather than a local invention. Cite https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/sec09/tech/full_papers/crosby.pdf. **No code to adopt** (the paper describes a structure; no licence for reuse of an implementation was located).
- **Do not adopt DVC.** Apache-2.0 would permit it, but its hash-mismatch policy is weaker than the project's (`dvc/repo/index.py:123`), so importing it would import the exact failure mode `newquant/ledger.py:96` exists to prevent. Record DVC v3.x (repo `treeverse/dvc`, branch `main`, HEAD as read on 2026-11-04) as a **rejected** comparison with that reason, per the method's outcome rules.
- **Remove C5 from the contribution list.** Report it as engineering that meets an invariant, and move the intellectual weight to the one thing it enables: net-CNY accounting that cannot be silently revised. That is the precondition for C4's result being trustworthy, not a result.
- The frame to keep: *the cost gate is evaluated where the order actually is, not where the model wanted it to be.* That is one sentence, it is correct, and it needs no novelty claim.

## Synthesis — what the five verdicts mean for the project

1. **One new object.** Every model cited here has a **never-empty** feasible set: continuous quantities, proportional costs, or a lot constraint handled inside an MILP that always has an answer. None can represent "this order, at this capital, is not admissible". The cleared-cell share at a given NAV (C4) is that object. Build the contribution there and nowhere else.
2. **Three re-descriptions.** C1's "breadth capped by the fee floor" is Baule's abstract sentence. C2's "few-parameter combiner" is Brandt–Santa-Clara–Valkanov. C5's "hash-chained append-only ledger" is Crosby–Wallach. None of these is a fault; presenting them as contributions is.
3. **One claim that contradicts its own citation.** C1's premise (fixed costs hurt small accounts most) is the opposite of Gârleanu–Pedersen's aside after eq. (10) — *"A larger absolute risk aversion can also be viewed as a smaller investor, for whom transaction costs play a smaller role"*. The project is right and GP is describing a different cost function, but the project must say so, because a referee who knows the dynamic-portfolio literature will raise exactly that sentence.
4. **One design choice is unsupported.** The 3-state grid's middle state has no basis in the fixed-cost theory, which predicts a **bulk trade to target** as the only non-zero action. Test {0, full} as the baseline.
5. **One number that should change the design.** Baule's Table 2 puts the optimum at **2–3 names** at this capital. The project runs 4 sleeves + cash. If 4 cannot clear the gate at 50k (it does not: 0.1% of cells), the project should say so as a *predicted* result from prior art, not as a discovery.
6. **The honest one-sentence contribution.** *At a 5 CNY commission floor with a 100-share lot, the fraction of (month × instrument) decisions that are executable at 50k CNY is 0.1% and at 100k CNY is 99.7%; this makes "not measurable at this capital" a verdict prior work does not report, and it implies a 2-state action set rather than the weight vector that continuous models assume.* Everything else in C1–C5 is scaffolding around that sentence.
